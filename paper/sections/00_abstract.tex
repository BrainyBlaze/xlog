\begin{abstract}
\noindent
xlog is a universal GPU-native engine for neurosymbolic integration: it couples neural perception with deterministic Datalog, probabilistic inference, and epistemic reasoning over world views on one CUDA runtime, with all semantic state device-resident. Prior systems bridge a network to one CPU-side symbolic backend; in xlog the network is an ordinary predicate, feeding every reasoning mode through one transfer-free mechanism. A GPU-native knowledge-compilation pipeline (provenance $\to$ CNF $\to$ Decision-DNNF $\to$ circuit) makes those outputs a differentiable circuit whose forward pass is exact weighted model counting and whose backward pass yields exact gradients; a Monte Carlo path covers programs too large to compile. An on-GPU CDCL solver proves every compiled circuit equivalent to its source formula, so the compilation back-end is machine-checked rather than assumed, and gradients return to PyTorch's autograd zero-copy through DLPack. Circuit caching yields a $2.74\times$ training speedup on MNIST addition, and a worst-case-optimal join subsystem a $27.96\times$ geometric-mean gain over xlog's own binary-join baseline. Against external engines the picture is mixed: MNIST addition trains $2.8\times$ faster per epoch than in Scallop at equal accuracy and skewed-graph triangle counting runs $12$--$42\times$ faster than Soufflé in megabytes where materialization exhausts the device, while exact inference is correctness-equivalent to but slower than ProbLog2. On a public video benchmark, a proximity predicate trained through symbolic credit alone---never shown a distance label---replaces hand-set geometry without loss of held-out accuracy. On a maritime corpus of thousands of gold events, weighted clauses beat crisp selection by $0.065$ F1, and a single chronological training pass reproduces that result exactly. The central contribution is the unified, transfer-free neurosymbolic architecture.
\end{abstract}
