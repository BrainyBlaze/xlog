\section{Rule Induction at Scale: Maritime \texttt{rendezVous}}\label{sec:maritime}

The previous section ends on an evidence problem: under a leak-free split, CAVIAR carries eleven observable \texttt{meeting} initiations, too few for any cross-validated statement about rule learning to mean anything. This section moves the same induction machinery to the second public corpus the dedicated Event-Calculus learners report on---the Brest AIS maritime stream~\cite{brestais,maritimecer}, where the target fluent \texttt{rendezVous} carries 3{,}548 gold intervals---and measures three things the CAVIAR corpus could not: a crisp rule search against a ceiling declared before the run, the gain from weighting the clauses of the same gated pool, and the cost of learning those weights in a single chronological pass. Each column was pre-registered before its own run, with its hypotheses, parameters and forbidden comparisons fixed in a document committed ahead of the run it governs; results were appended to those documents under their own no-silent-amendment rule, and every measured column below resolves to a committed, md5-pinned JSON artifact under \texttt{docs/experiments/maritime/results/}; the re-scoring diagnostics of the online column (weight-vector distances and score margins) are recorded in the pre-registration document from an independent reproduction of the shipped artifacts rather than shipped as JSON. With the third column the setting the published systems occupy---enumerated rule search, weighted clauses, single-pass online training---is covered on this corpus in its three training settings---enumerated rule search, weighted clauses, single-pass weight training. What the section does \emph{not} do, by its own pre-registration, is place our numbers in a table beside the published maritime figure; \Cref{subsec:maritime-comparison} says why.

\subsection{The Corpus, and What Its Labels Are}

The inputs are two public exports of the RTEC event-recognition pipeline~\cite{rtec} over the Brest AIS critical points: the composite-event archive (the pipeline's output, Zenodo record 2557290) and the critical-event archive (its input). Both are md5-pinned in the experiment's README, and a converter committed before any run reduces them to a pair-time corpus: \textbf{454{,}858} pair-time rows over \textbf{806} vessel pairs---\textbf{302} with at least one \texttt{rendezVous} interval and \textbf{504} negatives drawn from a pool of 2{,}014 proximity-only pairs by a deterministic stride---carrying \textbf{3{,}548} gold intervals and \textbf{3{,}579} positive rows, $0.79\%$ of all rows. The converter and the pair scope contain no random number generator at all; the corpus is a pure function of the two pinned archives, and a verifier re-derives its constants and structural invariants before each run and aborts on any mismatch; a separate committed verification report records the alignment of every gold interval with the relation streams.

Three properties of this corpus are declared in the pre-registration because each one bounds what a number on it can mean. First, \textbf{the supervision is synthetic}. The gold \texttt{rendezVous} labels are the output of a hand-crafted RTEC rule evaluated over the same interval streams the relation vocabulary is derived from; the verifier's alignment fractions of $1.0$ are therefore conversion fidelity, not label validity. The published learners share this regime---the WOLED paper notes that the maritime ground truth is synthetic and that its learning curves are consequently alike across algorithms~\cite{woled}---and the task every system on this corpus measures is the \emph{reconstruction of a known rule}, here under a vocabulary that does not contain all of it. Second, \textbf{the vocabulary is incomplete by construction}. It holds eleven unary pair-time relations fixed by the converter (\texttt{proximity}, \texttt{both\_lowspeed}, \texttt{both\_stopped\_far}, \texttt{both\_open\_sea}, \texttt{became\_proximate}, and so on) and omits the gold rule's two remaining discriminators: its $240$\,s minimum-duration threshold and its exclusion of tug and pilot pairs. Perfect reconstruction inside the base vocabulary is impossible, and the pre-registration says so before the first run. Third, \textbf{the temporal grid is sparse}. The corpus has ${\approx}1.009$ positive rows per gold interval---the covering row is almost always the interval's own start boundary---so pointwise F1 and interval F1 coincide on it, and it is not the dense per-timepoint grid on which the published maritime figures were computed. The pre-registration therefore forbids comparing any number in this section with the published $0.98$, and forbids comparing pass times with the published training times; both prohibitions are honoured below.

\subsection{Protocol}

\textbf{Metric and declared ceiling.} The primary metric is pointwise F1 on the positive class---never accuracy, which the empty predictor saturates at this class balance---reported per held-out fold with the median and spread of per-fold F1 beside the micro-average over pooled counts, and accompanied by an interval-level F1 under a fixed matching definition. The pre-registration declared a ceiling of ${\approx}0.66$ before the baseline ran, with its mechanism: the gold rule's definitional body, \texttt{proximity \& both\_low\_or\_stopped \& both\_open\_sea}, reaches recall $1.0$ at pointwise precision ${\approx}0.49$, because the two inexpressible discriminators are what separate its false positives from its true ones. A committed probe later derived that operating point from the pinned archives---$3{,}579$ true positives, $3{,}689$ false positives, no false negative, F1 $0.6599$---and decomposed the false positives: $733$ lie in negative pairs (the pair-exclusion class), $2{,}956$ lie in predicted runs shorter than $240$\,s, and \emph{none} falls outside the two pre-identified discriminators.

\textbf{Folds are vessel pairs.} A pair's rows are never split across folds, because the top pair alone carries $33.4\%$ of all positive rows and the top four carry $60.2\%$; any row- or interval-level split would leak the dominant encounter across the train/test boundary. Five folds (not CAVIAR's ten, because of that concentration) are assigned by a deterministic greedy longest-processing-time rule with no seed, stratified by positive mass, and the assignment guarantees that no fold differs from another by more than the largest single pair's own count. The consequence is disclosed rather than smoothed: held-out fold 0 is the top pair plus one negative pair, so per-fold figures are necessarily unequal and the micro number is never reported alone.

\textbf{Search and gate as on CAVIAR.} The crisp column runs the same relational sequential-covering search as \Cref{sec:ecinduction}---bodies of at most three literals, at most four clauses, per-fold held-out F1 for candidate selection, a minimum of two newly covered positives per clause, and the permutation-null fit gate: $1{,}000$ label permutations per fold (seed $7$, the one seed in the protocol, which also drives the inner holdout), threshold at the $95$th percentile of the pool-maximum per-fold F1, derived on the training side. The target is the direct per-row positive label; Event-Calculus induction through inertia is out of scope on this grid, for the reason given above (there are almost no interior rows for inertia to fill). All searches and all weight trainings in this section are deterministic CPU procedures; the GPU engine is not exercised here, and the artifacts' pinned input paths identify the host (see \Cref{sec:eval}).

\subsection{Three Pre-Registered Columns}

\textbf{Crisp search (baseline).} Micro pointwise F1 $\mathbf{0.6746}$---precision $0.5109$, recall $0.9925$ ($3{,}552$ true positives, $3{,}400$ false positives, $27$ false negatives); interval F1 $0.6772$ with $3{,}545$ of the $3{,}548$ gold intervals matched; per-fold F1 $0.9933 / 0.4690 / 0.6685 / 0.5699 / 0.6596$, median $0.6596$. On every fold the search reconstructs the gold body decomposed into its two disjunctive branches as separate clauses---\texttt{both\_open\_sea \&\allowbreak both\_stopped\_far \&\allowbreak proximity} and \texttt{both\_lowspeed \&\allowbreak both\_open\_sea \&\allowbreak proximity}---and stops for the honest reasons (no positives left in the residual, or abstention on the residual tail, attributed to ties only indirectly); no clause is ever committed below its fold's null gate. The aggregate lands where the declared ceiling mechanism predicted: precision near one half at recall near one, with $27$ of $3{,}579$ positive rows missed. The learned operator is not literally the definitional body---its precision is $0.5109$ against the body's $0.4924$---so the reading is about the mechanism, not operator identity. The fold spread ($0.469$--$0.993$) is the declared pair-concentration effect: the fold holding the top pair's long clean encounters scores $0.993$, and the folds of many small pairs concentrate the duration-threshold false positives. By the pre-registration's own reading, any improvement past this column must come from expressiveness, not from tuning the search.

\textbf{Weighted clauses over the same gated pool (A-soft).} The second column keeps the vocabulary, folds and gate of the first and changes one thing: instead of selecting clauses crisply it weights them. The body pool is every conjunction of one to three literals that passes the \emph{same} per-fold permutation-null gate procedure (thresholds re-derived per column over its own pool); each body carries one weight; a row's score is the noisy-OR $1-\prod_c\bigl(1-\sigma(w_c)\,\mathrm{cover}_c\bigr)$---the semantics of the engine's relational real-credit columns of \Cref{sec:nesy}---trained by binary cross-entropy with Adam ($300$ steps, learning rate $0.05$, every weight initialised to $-2.0$ so that every clause starts off) and thresholded at $0.5$. The pre-registered hypothesis expected this column to stay within $[0.66, 0.70]$, on the reasoning that the ceiling is a property of the vocabulary. \textbf{It was rejected in the favourable direction}: micro F1 $\mathbf{0.7398}$, $+0.0652$ over the crisp column on the same vocabulary, folds and gate, at precision $0.8715$ and recall $0.6426$ ($2{,}300 / 339 / 1{,}279$), interval F1 $0.7435$, per-fold F1 $0.9346 / 0.4237 / 0.8000 / 0.6928 / 0.5342$ (its weakest fold, $0.4237$, sits below the crisp column's $0.4690$---the pre-registration's reason for never reading micro F1 alone), per-fold median $0.6928$ against $0.6596$. The expectation error is instructive and is recorded as such: the ``$0.66$ ceiling'' is the F1 of the definitional body's \emph{operating point}---crisp reconstruction at recall near one---and weights are not confined to that point; training moved it to high precision at lower recall, which carries more F1. The per-fold picture is stated whole: the weighted column wins the micro figure and the median but only two of the five individual folds ($0.8000$ against $0.6685$, $0.6928$ against $0.5699$), and the crisp column keeps the other three. Weights over the same clauses beating crisp selection is the published OLED-to-WOLED delta; here it is measured as an isolated variable on a corpus with $3{,}548$ gold intervals rather than eleven events.

\textbf{The duration vocabulary (Arm B).} A third and fourth run add one relation, \texttt{sustained\_240}: a row carries it when it lies in a half-open component of the continuous intersection $\texttt{proximity}\cap\texttt{both\_low\_or\_stopped}\cap\texttt{both\_open\_sea}$ whose duration is at least $240$\,s. The constant is the gold generator's own; adding it is language parity with RTEC, not a look at the data. The pre-registration required the new ceiling to be derived by the committed probe \emph{before} any cross-validated run and declared the probe's output canonical; the expectation written down beforehand was ${\approx}0.907$, on the assumption that duration removes only the $2{,}956$ short-run false positives. The canon came out higher---$3{,}579$ true positives, $22$ false positives, no false negative, F1 $0.9969$---because the duration discriminator also eliminates at least $711$ of the $733$ negative-pair false positives: the excluded pairs' body-condition episodes are themselves almost always shorter than $240$\,s on the continuous streams. Two consequences were recorded before the runs: the pair-exclusion discriminator is largely redundant with duration on this corpus, a finding rather than an assumption; and Arm~B operates at saturation, so the informative weights-versus-crisp arena is Arm~A. The runs confirmed both. Crisp search with the widened vocabulary lands exactly on the canon ($0.9969$, precision $0.9939$, recall $1.0$, median $0.9950$), and the weighted column equals it on every fold: the pre-registered directional hypothesis (weighted $\geq$ crisp on the median) holds trivially, with no headroom for weights to add anything. The B-column artifacts were regenerated once, after review found that the relation had admitted a component's right endpoint against the converter's half-open contract; the correction removed one false positive ($23\to22$), and the pre-registration document records it rather than absorbing it. That both columns saturate once the duration discriminator is expressible mirrors the published maritime setting, where every reported system reaches the same figure on its own grid.

\textbf{Single-pass online weights (O-online).} The last column takes the weighted column and changes only the training regime. The weights are learned in \emph{one} pass over the training fold in ascending global time---all of the fold's pairs interleaved, as in a stream---in mini-batches of $1{,}000$ rows with one Adam step per batch, at the batch column's learning rate of $0.05$ rather than WOLED's AdaGrad, so that exactly one variable (batch weights against single-pass weights) separates the two columns; the decision to keep the optimiser is declared. The body pool and its gate are those of the batch column, computed on the training side; the pre-registration calls the frame ``semi-online'' for that reason and claims no online structure learning. The column runs on the base vocabulary only, because \texttt{sustained\_240} at time $t$ uses the \emph{future} duration of its interval---a leak in an online setting, found in reconnaissance and excluded by construction. Three hypotheses were fixed: degradation against the batch column of at most $0.04$ in micro F1 (a figure below $0.70$ was to be published as a negative), a reverse-chronological pass within $0.02$ of the forward one, and a decreasing prequential error along the stream. \textbf{The measured degradation is zero.} The single pass reproduces the batch column's thresholded predictions exactly---identical true-positive, false-positive and false-negative counts on every fold, micro F1 $0.7398$; the reverse pass is identical as well; and the prequential error rate falls from the first half of the stream to the second on all five folds in both orders (forward, fold 0: $0.0078\to0.0036$), under a first-half/second-half criterion that was written down at reporting time and is labelled as such.

The identity of predictions is a finding about the decision structure, not evidence that the online path fell through to the batch one, and the artifacts settle which: the learned weights differ substantially between regimes (fold 0's top body carries $\sigma(w)=0.6476$ in batch, $0.5880$ in the forward pass, $0.8398$ in the reverse pass; the $L_\infty$ distance between the logit vectors of any two regimes is $1.06$--$2.95$), and the online fold records carry the stream order, the window count, the prequential curve and the pass wall-time. The mechanism is simpler than ``robustness to the training regime''. In all fifteen fold$\times$regime cases exactly one body has $\sigma(w)>0.5$---always \texttt{became\_proximate \& both\_open\_sea \& both\_stopped\_far}---and the second-ranked body stays at $0.15$--$0.46$, so the noisy-OR of all the others never reaches the threshold on any held-out row: all three trainings reduce to the same crisp rule, and the identity of the predictions is the identity of that rule, not closeness of the weight vectors. It is also fragile. Re-scoring the held-out folds with the shipped weights (an independent reproduction that recovered every artifact bitwise) puts the highest score of a row \emph{not} covered by the top body at $0.493$ in fold 1 under the batch weights, $0.007$ below the threshold, with $1{,}270$ rows of that fold between $0.3$ and $0.5$; a small change of learning rate, step count or initialisation could push the second body over the threshold and move the counts by hundreds of rows in either direction. The honest scope is therefore the pre-registered one: prediction identity holds for this pool, corpus and threshold, hangs on the discrete structure of the thresholded decision, and is not a general theorem.

\subsection{Comparison, Not Ranking}\label{subsec:maritime-comparison}

The published figure for \texttt{rendezVous} on this corpus is $0.98$ for OLED, WOLED-MLN and WOLED-ASP alike, with theories of eighteen literals, measured on a train/test split of halves~\cite{woled}; a second published experiment on a six-sequence fragment of the corpus compares WOLED-ASP with batch learners and reports figures from $0.91$ to $0.97$ in a bar chart. The pre-registration forbids placing any of these beside the rows of \Cref{tab:maritime}, and the reason is not modesty but axis. Those numbers live on a dense per-timepoint grid with inertia filling the interior of every interval; ours live on a critical-point grid with ${\approx}1.009$ positive rows per interval, where pointwise and interval F1 coincide and a per-frame reading of the published protocol is not reproduced. A split of halves is not a pair-atom five-fold; a row of synthetic labels is a row of synthetic labels on either side. The numbers do not measure the same thing, and we do not tabulate them together. What \emph{is} comparable is in kind rather than in magnitude, and two such correspondences are worth stating. The published systems saturate on this corpus---every system in the full-corpus table reports the same figure---and so do ours once the duration discriminator is expressible, at a ceiling committed before the run. And the mechanism the published line reports as the move from OLED to WOLED on CAVIAR, weights over the same clauses beating crisp selection, is reproduced here as $+0.0652$ micro F1 with everything else held fixed, on a corpus large enough for that difference to be a measurement rather than an anecdote---and it is reproduced a second time, without loss, when the weights are learned in one pass.

\subsection{What This Establishes, and What It Does Not}

It establishes four things. A crisp relational search under the CAVIAR gates reconstructs the gold rule's body on every fold of a corpus more than two orders of magnitude richer in positive events, and lands where a ceiling declared before the run said it would, with the ceiling's false-positive mass decomposed by a committed tool into exactly the two discriminators the vocabulary omits. Weighting the clauses of the same gated pool raises micro F1 by $0.0652$ and the per-fold median by $0.0332$ on the same folds, against a pre-registered expectation that it would not---a negative result for the hypothesis and a positive one for the mechanism, stated with its fold-level nuance (two of five folds). Adding the duration discriminator saturates both columns at a canon committed beforehand, and shows the pair-exclusion discriminator to be largely redundant with duration on this corpus. And the weights learned in one chronological pass reproduce the batch predictions exactly, for a reason that is named---a single body above threshold in every regime---rather than described as robustness, together with the $0.007$ margin that makes it fragile.

It does not establish a ranking against OLED or WOLED on \texttt{rendezVous}, and none is claimed; the grids differ and the pre-registration forbids the table. It does not establish anything about detecting rendezvous in the wild: the labels are a hand-crafted rule's output, so every number here is a rule-reconstruction figure under an incomplete vocabulary, and nothing more. It does not demonstrate online structure learning---the pool and its gate are computed on the training side---nor a general robustness of thresholded noisy-OR predictors to the training regime. It exercises none of the GPU substrate: every search and every weight training in this section is a deterministic CPU procedure, and the artifacts say so. What it does show is that the induction protocol of \Cref{sec:ecinduction}---pre-registered gates, pair-level folds, permutation nulls, committed artifacts---carries unchanged to a corpus where its verdicts are measurements rather than anecdotes, and that on that corpus the two mechanisms the published line of Event-Calculus learners is built on, weighted clauses and single-pass training, are measured in isolation and behave as their authors report.
