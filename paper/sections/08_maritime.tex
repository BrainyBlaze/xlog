\section{Rule Induction at Scale:\texorpdfstring{\\}{ }Maritime Rendezvous}\label{sec:maritime}

The previous section ends on an evidence problem: under a leak-free split, CAVIAR carries eleven observable \texttt{meeting} initiations, too few for any cross-validated statement about rule learning to mean anything. This section moves the same induction machinery to the second public corpus that the dedicated Event-Calculus learners report on: the Brest AIS maritime stream~\cite{brestais,maritimecer}, where the target fluent \texttt{rendezVous}---two vessels close together, stopped or moving slowly, in open water---carries 3{,}548 gold intervals. It asks three questions the CAVIAR corpus could not answer. Does a \emph{crisp} rule search---one that selects clauses and lets each fire with weight one, as in \Cref{sec:ecinduction}---land where a ceiling declared before the run said it would? How much is gained by \emph{weighting} the clauses of the same gated pool instead of selecting them? And what does it cost to learn those weights in a single chronological pass over the stream? Six runs answer them (\Cref{tab:maritime}): crisp and weighted search on the base vocabulary, the same pair on a widened vocabulary, and two single-pass variants of the weighted run. With the last two, the three training settings of the published systems---enumerated rule search, weighted clauses, single-pass weight training---are all covered on this corpus. Every run's hypotheses, parameters and forbidden comparisons were fixed before it was executed, with one exception marked where it arises. One consequence is visible throughout: we do not place our numbers beside the published maritime figure, and \Cref{subsec:maritime-comparison} says why.

\subsection{The Corpus, and What Its Labels Are}

The inputs are two public exports of the RTEC event-recognition system~\cite{rtec}, a hand-written Event-Calculus rule engine, over the Brest AIS stream. One is the composite events it recognised (Zenodo record 2557290). The other is its input, the \emph{critical points} of the compressed trajectories~\cite{maritimecer}: the timestamps at which a vessel's movement changes---a stop, a turn, a change of speed, a gap in its signal---the only temporal grid the corpus carries. A deterministic converter reduces the two into a \emph{pair-time corpus}: one row per (vessel pair, timestamp), carrying eleven relations that either hold or do not for that pair at that time, and the gold label. The corpus has \textbf{454{,}858} rows over \textbf{806} vessel pairs---\textbf{302} with at least one \texttt{rendezVous} interval and \textbf{504} negatives drawn by a fixed stride from the 2{,}014 proximity-only pairs (pairs that come within proximity but carry no gold interval)---with \textbf{3{,}548} gold intervals and \textbf{3{,}579} positive rows, $0.79\%$ of all rows. Neither the converter nor the choice of pairs uses randomness: the corpus is a pure function of the two archives.

Three properties of this corpus bound what a number on it can mean. First, \textbf{the supervision is synthetic}. The gold \texttt{rendezVous} labels are the output of a hand-crafted RTEC rule evaluated over the same interval streams the relation vocabulary is derived from; that every gold interval aligns perfectly with those streams therefore says that the conversion is faithful, not that the labels are right. The published learners share this regime: the WOLED paper notes that the maritime ground truth is synthetic and that its learning curves are consequently alike across algorithms~\cite{woled}. So the task every system on this corpus measures is the \emph{reconstruction of a known rule}, here under a vocabulary that does not contain all of it. Second, \textbf{the vocabulary is incomplete by construction}. It holds eleven pair-time relations fixed by the converter (\texttt{proximity}, \texttt{both\_lowspeed}, \texttt{both\_stopped\_far}, \texttt{both\_open\_sea}, \texttt{became\_proximate}, and so on) and omits the gold rule's two remaining discriminators: its $240$\,s minimum-duration threshold and its exclusion of tug and pilot pairs. Perfect reconstruction inside the base vocabulary is therefore impossible. Third, \textbf{the temporal grid is sparse}. The corpus has ${\approx}1.009$ positive rows per gold interval---the covering row is almost always the interval's own start boundary---so pointwise F1 and interval F1 coincide on it. It is far sparser than the grid on which the published maritime figures were computed (the critical points of every vessel, with inertia filling the interior of each interval), which is why no number in this section is compared with them (\Cref{subsec:maritime-comparison}).

\subsection{Protocol}

\textbf{Metric and declared ceiling.} The primary metric is pointwise F1 on the positive class, that is, F1 over individual rows; never accuracy, which the empty predictor saturates at this class balance. We report it per held-out fold, with the median and spread of the per-fold values beside the micro-average over pooled counts. An interval-level figure accompanies it: a gold interval counts as matched when at least one predicted row falls inside it within the same episode of the pair's timeline, and symmetrically for predicted intervals. A ceiling was declared before the first run, together with the mechanism that sets it. The gold rule's definitional body is \texttt{proximity \& both\_low\_or\_stopped \& both\_open\_sea}, where \texttt{both\_low\_or\_stopped} is the disjunction of \texttt{both\_lowspeed} and \texttt{both\_stopped\_far}; all three are relations of the vocabulary, so the search may express the body either through the disjunction itself or as its two branches. That body reaches recall $1.0$ at pointwise precision ${\approx}0.49$, hence F1 ${\approx}0.66$, because the two inexpressible discriminators are exactly what separate its false positives from its true ones. A direct evaluation of the gold rule's own body on the archives---the \emph{ceiling probe}---gives that operating point exactly: $3{,}579$ true positives, $3{,}689$ false positives, no false negative, F1 $0.6599$. It also decomposes the false positives: $733$ lie in negative pairs (the pair-exclusion class), $2{,}956$ lie in predicted runs shorter than $240$\,s, and \emph{none} falls outside the two pre-identified discriminators.

\textbf{Folds are vessel pairs.} A pair's rows are never split across folds, because the positive mass is concentrated in a few pairs. The top pair alone carries $33.4\%$ of all positive rows and the top four carry $60.2\%$, so any row- or interval-level split would leak the dominant encounter across the train/test boundary. Five folds (not CAVIAR's ten, because of that concentration) are assigned by a deterministic greedy longest-processing-time rule with no seed, stratified by positive mass; no fold differs from another by more than the largest single pair's own count. The consequence is disclosed rather than smoothed: held-out fold 0 is the top pair plus one negative pair, so per-fold figures are necessarily unequal and the micro number is never reported alone.

\textbf{Search and gate as on CAVIAR.} The crisp run uses the same relational sequential-covering search as \Cref{sec:ecinduction}: clauses are added one at a time, each scored on the positives the previous ones left uncovered. Its limits are those of the CAVIAR runs: bodies of at most three literals, at most four clauses, per-fold held-out F1 for candidate selection, a minimum of two newly covered positives per clause. So is its permutation-null fit gate: $1{,}000$ label permutations per fold, threshold at the $95$th percentile of the pool-maximum per-fold F1, derived on the training side. The protocol has one seed, $7$, which drives the permutations and the candidate-selection holdout of \Cref{sec:ecinduction}. The target is the direct per-row positive label. Event-Calculus induction through inertia is out of scope on this grid, for the reason given above: there are almost no interior rows for inertia to fill. All searches and all weight trainings in this section are deterministic CPU procedures; the GPU engine is not exercised here.

\subsection{Six Runs for Three Questions}

\Cref{tab:maritime} collects the six runs; the paragraphs below take them in turn, each against the hypothesis we fixed for it beforehand.

\textbf{Crisp search, base vocabulary.} The first run is the CAVIAR search unchanged, and it lands where the ceiling mechanism predicted: precision near one half at recall near one. Micro pointwise F1 is $\mathbf{0.6746}$ (precision $0.5109$, recall $0.9925$; $3{,}552$ true positives, $3{,}400$ false positives, $27$ of the $3{,}579$ positive rows missed), and interval F1 is $0.6772$ with $3{,}545$ of the $3{,}548$ gold intervals matched. The per-fold F1 values are $0.9933 / 0.4690 / 0.6685 / 0.5699 / 0.6596$, median $0.6596$. On every fold the search commits both disjunctive branches of the gold body, one clause each---\texttt{both\_open\_sea \&\allowbreak\ both\_stopped\_far \&\allowbreak\ proximity} and \texttt{both\_lowspeed \&\allowbreak\ both\_open\_sea \&\allowbreak\ proximity}. Three folds add one further clause (on two of them the disjunction relation itself, which subsumes both branches). The search then stops either because no positives remain uncovered or because the selection abstains on the few that do; no clause is ever accepted below its fold's null gate. The learned operator is not literally the definitional body---its precision is $0.5109$ against the body's $0.4924$---so the reading is about the mechanism, not operator identity. The fold spread ($0.469$--$0.993$) is the pair-concentration effect described above: the fold holding the top pair's long clean encounters scores $0.993$, while the folds of many small pairs concentrate the duration-threshold false positives. Any improvement past this run must therefore come from expressiveness, not from tuning the search.

\textbf{Weighted clauses, same pool, base vocabulary.} The second run keeps the vocabulary, folds and gate of the first and changes one thing: instead of selecting clauses crisply, it weights them. The body pool is every conjunction of one to three literals that passes the same per-fold permutation-null gate procedure (thresholds re-derived per run over its own pool), and each body carries one weight. A row's score is the noisy-OR $1-\prod_c\bigl(1-\sigma(w_c)\,\mathrm{cover}_c\bigr)$---the mixture of \Cref{subsec:trainable-bodies}, here with every clause purely relational---trained by binary cross-entropy with Adam and thresholded at $0.5$. Training runs $300$ steps at learning rate $0.05$ from every weight at $-2.0$, so that every clause starts switched off ($\sigma(-2)\approx0.12$). We expected this run to stay within $[0.66, 0.70]$, on the reasoning that the ceiling is a property of the vocabulary. \textbf{The hypothesis was rejected in the favourable direction.} Micro F1 is $\mathbf{0.7398}$, $+0.0652$ over the crisp run on the same vocabulary, folds and gate, at precision $0.8715$ and recall $0.6426$ ($2{,}300 / 339 / 1{,}279$); interval F1 is $0.7435$; the per-fold values are $0.9346 / 0.4237 / 0.8000 / 0.6928 / 0.5342$, median $0.6928$ against the crisp $0.6596$. The expectation was wrong for an instructive reason. The ``$0.66$ ceiling'' is the F1 of the definitional body's operating point---crisp reconstruction at recall near one---and weights are not confined to that point: training moved it to high precision at lower recall, which carries more F1. Fold by fold, the picture is mixed. The weighted run wins the micro figure and the median but only two of the five individual folds ($0.8000$ against $0.6685$, $0.6928$ against $0.5699$), and the crisp run keeps the other three; its weakest fold, $0.4237$, sits below the crisp run's $0.4690$, which is why micro F1 is never read alone here. Weights over the same clauses beating crisp selection is the gain the published line reports between OLED and its successor WOLED---which adds a weight to every clause, in an MLN- or an ASP-based variant---and attributes to weighting~\cite{woled}; here it is measured as an isolated variable on a corpus with $3{,}548$ gold intervals rather than eleven events.

\textbf{Both runs again with the duration relation.} The third and fourth runs add one relation, \texttt{sustained\_240}: a row carries it when the conjunction $\texttt{proximity}\cap\texttt{both\_low\_or\_stopped}\cap\texttt{both\_open\_sea}$ holds continuously through a stretch of at least $240$\,s that contains the row (a half-open component of the continuous streams). The constant is the gold generator's own; adding it is language parity with RTEC, not a look at the data. The new ceiling was derived by the ceiling probe \emph{before} any cross-validated run, against an expectation of ${\approx}0.907$ fixed beforehand on the assumption that duration removes only the $2{,}956$ short-run false positives. The ceiling came out higher---$3{,}579$ true positives, $22$ false positives, no false negative, F1 $0.9969$---because the duration discriminator also eliminates at least $711$ of the $733$ negative-pair false positives: the excluded pairs' body-condition episodes are themselves almost always shorter than $240$\,s on the continuous streams. Two consequences follow, both fixed before the runs: the pair-exclusion discriminator is largely redundant with duration on this corpus, a finding rather than an assumption; and the duration vocabulary operates at saturation, so the informative comparison of weighted against crisp is the one on the base vocabulary. Both held. Crisp search with the widened vocabulary lands exactly on the ceiling derived before the run ($0.9969$, precision $0.9939$, recall $1.0$, median $0.9950$), and the weighted run equals it on every fold; the directional hypothesis fixed in advance (weighted $\geq$ crisp on the median) holds trivially, with no headroom for weights to add anything. That both runs saturate once the duration discriminator is expressible mirrors the published maritime setting, where every system in the full-corpus table reaches the same figure on its own grid.

\textbf{Weighted clauses learned in a single pass.} The last two runs take the weighted run and change only how its weights are trained. Instead of $300$ Adam steps over the whole training fold, the weights are learned in \emph{one} pass over the fold in ascending global time---all of the fold's pairs interleaved, as in a stream---in mini-batches of $1{,}000$ rows with one Adam step per batch, at the same learning rate of $0.05$. Exactly one variable therefore separates the two regimes, batch weights against single-pass weights. We kept Adam rather than switching to WOLED's AdaGrad for the same reason. The body pool and its gate are those of the batch run, computed on the training side, so this is single-pass \emph{weight} learning---``semi-online'', in that only the weights see the stream---and not online structure learning. It uses the base vocabulary only: \texttt{sustained\_240} at time $t$ depends on how long the current interval will turn out to last, a leak in a streaming setting, and was excluded before the run. Three hypotheses were fixed beforehand. The single pass should lose at most $0.04$ micro F1 against the batch run (below $0.70$ we would have reported a negative result); a pass in \emph{reverse} chronological order should land within $0.02$ of the forward one; and the prequential error---the error on each batch, scored before the batch is trained on---should fall along the stream. \textbf{The measured degradation is zero.} The single pass reproduces the batch run's thresholded predictions exactly: the same true-positive, false-positive and false-negative counts on every fold and, as re-scoring the held-out folds with the stored weights confirms, the same decision on every row, for micro F1 $0.7398$. The reverse pass is identical as well, and the prequential error falls from the first half of the stream to the second on all five folds in both orders---halves being the one criterion of these runs settled as the results were written up rather than before them (forward, fold 0: $0.0078\to0.0036$).

Identical predictions are a statement about the decision structure, not evidence that the single-pass path silently fell through to the batch one: the learned weights differ substantially between regimes. Fold 0's top body carries $\sigma(w)=0.6476$ in batch, $0.5880$ in the forward pass and $0.8398$ in the reverse pass, and the $L_\infty$ distance between the logit vectors of any two regimes is $1.06$--$2.95$. The mechanism is simpler than ``robustness to the training regime''. In all fifteen fold$\times$regime cases exactly one body has $\sigma(w)>0.5$---always \texttt{became\_proximate \&\allowbreak\ both\_open\_sea \&\allowbreak\ both\_stopped\_far}---and the second-ranked body stays at $0.15$--$0.46$, so the noisy-OR of all the others never reaches the threshold on any held-out row. All three trainings therefore reduce to the same crisp rule, and the predictions agree because that rule does, not because the weight vectors are close. The agreement is also fragile. Re-scoring the held-out folds with the stored weights puts the highest score of a row \emph{not} covered by the top body at $0.493$ in fold 1 under the batch weights, $0.007$ below the threshold, with $1{,}270$ rows of that fold between $0.3$ and $0.5$. A small change of learning rate, step count or initialisation could push the second body over the threshold and move the counts by hundreds of rows in either direction. The scope is therefore the one we fixed in advance: prediction identity holds for this pool, corpus and threshold, hangs on the discrete structure of the thresholded decision, and is not a general theorem.

\subsection{Comparison, Not Ranking}\label{subsec:maritime-comparison}

The published figure for \texttt{rendezVous} on this corpus is $0.98$ for OLED and both variants of WOLED (MLN- and ASP-based) alike, with theories of eighteen literals, measured on a train/test split of halves~\cite{woled}. A second published experiment on a six-sequence fragment of the corpus compares WOLED-ASP with batch learners and reports figures from $0.91$ to $0.97$ in a bar chart. We fixed in advance that none of these would be placed beside the rows of \Cref{tab:maritime}, and the reason is not modesty: the two sets of numbers are not on one axis. Those numbers live on a dense grid---the critical points of every vessel, with inertia filling the interior of every interval; ours live on a per-pair grid with ${\approx}1.009$ positive rows per interval, where pointwise and interval F1 coincide, and we do not reproduce the published per-timepoint reading on it. A split of halves is not a pair-level five-fold cross-validation. For the same reason we do not compare pass times with the published training times. What \emph{is} comparable is in kind rather than in magnitude, and two such correspondences are worth stating. The published systems saturate on their preprocessing of the same stream, and so do ours once the duration discriminator is expressible, at a ceiling derived before the run. And the gain the published line reports between OLED and WOLED on CAVIAR, and attributes to weighting, is reproduced here as $+0.0652$ micro F1 with everything else held fixed---weights over the same clauses against crisp selection of them---on a corpus with $3{,}548$ gold intervals rather than eleven events. It is reproduced a second time, without loss, when the weights are learned in one pass.

\subsection{What This Establishes, and What It Does Not}

It establishes four things. A crisp relational search under the CAVIAR gates reconstructs the gold rule's body on every fold of a corpus more than two orders of magnitude richer in positive events, and lands where a ceiling declared before the run said it would, with the ceiling's false-positive mass decomposed by the ceiling probe into exactly the two discriminators the vocabulary omits. Weighting the clauses of the same gated pool raises micro F1 by $0.0652$ and the per-fold median by $0.0332$ on the same folds, against our expectation, fixed in advance, that it would not---a negative result for the hypothesis and a positive one for the mechanism, stated with its fold-level nuance (two of five folds). Adding the duration discriminator saturates both runs at a ceiling derived beforehand, and shows the pair-exclusion discriminator to be largely redundant with duration on this corpus. And the weights learned in one chronological pass reproduce the batch predictions exactly, for a reason that is named---a single body above threshold in every regime---rather than described as robustness, together with the $0.007$ margin that makes it fragile.

It does not establish a ranking against OLED or WOLED on \texttt{rendezVous}, and none is claimed: the grids differ (\Cref{subsec:maritime-comparison}). It does not establish anything about detecting rendezvous in the wild: the labels are a hand-crafted rule's output, so every number here is a rule-reconstruction figure under an incomplete vocabulary, and nothing more. It does not demonstrate online structure learning---the pool and its gate are computed on the training side---nor a general robustness of thresholded noisy-OR predictors to the training regime. It exercises none of the GPU substrate: every search and every weight training in this section is a deterministic CPU procedure. What it does show is that the induction protocol of \Cref{sec:ecinduction}---declared gates, pair-level folds, permutation nulls---carries, with its gates intact, to a corpus where its verdicts are measurements rather than anecdotes. On that corpus the two mechanisms the published line of Event-Calculus learners is built on, weighted clauses and single-pass training, are measured in isolation and behave as their authors report.
